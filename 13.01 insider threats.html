The Three Profiles

An insider threat is the risk that someone with authorised access uses it — wittingly or unwittingly — to harm the organisation. Asking "what is an insider threat?" is the wrong question. You must ask: which of the three is it?

Wants to do harm. Knows what they are doing.

Intent: Deliberate Harm

🎯

Motive / Triggers

Revenge, money, ideology, ego. Missed promotion, firing, recruitment by competitor.

📊

Volume & Cost

Small volume, but enormous cost (~US $4.99M average per incident according to IBM 2024).

🛡️

Defence Focus

UEBA on resignation/firing windows, Just-in-time access, Tight off-boarding.

Recognizing Red Flags

Singles are gossip. Combinations are cases. The bell rings when categories cross over within a tight time window.

👤 Behavioural Flags

Observed by managers, HR. Not the SIEM.

  • Sudden disgruntlement
  • Resignation announced in last 60 days (Highest risk: last 30 days)
  • Severe personal-life stressors

💻 Technical Flags

Observed by DLP, UEBA, IAM.

  • Mass downloads far above user baseline
  • Outbound uploads to personal cloud
  • First-time use of dormant high-privilege account (Bright red flag)

The Defence Stack

Three pillars. Each one watches a different thing. No pillar alone is enough. The SIEM glues them together.

DLP

Watches Data

Matches content against policy (PII, Source code). Verbs: block, quarantine, alert.

UEBA

Watches People

Learns user baseline, scores deviations. Flags relative behaviour using z-scores.

IAM / Access

Watches Rights

Enforces least privilege. Dormant high privilege is a top 3 incident predictor.

UEBA Anomaly Detection (The Diego Case)

z-score = (today - mean) / stdev. A z-score ≥ 3 triggers an alert.

The Investigation Panel

The moment an alert points at a named human, every subsequent action must pass three gates. No single function gets to fire alone.

  • Documented lawful basis to monitor?
  • Did employee accept monitoring (contract / banner)?
  • Action permitted by applicable law (GDPR, PIPEDA, CCPA)?
  • Evidence chain of custody maintained?
  • Counsel signed off in writing.
  • Is the investigation proportional to suspicion?
  • Least-intrusive method that still answers the question?
  • Bias check — are we scrutinising one group disproportionately?
  • Reversible until guilt is established?
  • Dignity of the employee preserved at every step?
  • Performance record reviewed — outlier or pattern?
  • EAP (Employee Assistance Programme) considered as first option?
  • Disciplinary procedure followed step by step?
  • Manager briefed need-to-know only — and told not to confront.

A fair process protects the innocent silently.

If any gate fails, you do not escalate. The manager joins the panel later, on legal advice.

The Insider Threat Program

An ITP is not a tool. It is a sustained program built on three layers.

1. Governance (Top)

One named executive (CISO, CRO) owns the charter, budget, and board reporting. Boring rituals keep it funded.

2. Operations (Middle)

Five verbs: Define (crown jewels), Detect (tools), Assess (the panel), Manage (intervene), Learn (post-incident review).

3. Culture (Foundation)

Trust + verify. Psychological safety. A "just-culture" model where mistakes are coached and only deliberate harm is punished. Wellbeing & EAP are cheaper than incidents.
